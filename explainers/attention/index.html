Which Harry?

Two sentences, one shared word, two different meanings. Nothing about the word itself says which — so the model builds its meaning out of the company it keeps. This is real attention, measured from BERT, not an illustration.

Reading

Every cell is one attention head, and this is all of them — 12 layers of 12. Click any one to look through it. You are currently in layer 3, head 1.

Up is later in the computation. Each layer rewrites every word using the layer below, so layer 12 works on words already rewritten eleven times. The layers are steps, and their order matters.

Across is not a sequence. The twelve heads in a layer run at once, never see each other, and are combined at the end of the layer. "Head 7" is a slot number, not a stage.

So there is no final picture. The top right corner is not an answer: layer 12 is the last mixing step, head 12 a slot. What the model hands on is not an attention map but a vector for every word — which is what the chart at the bottom measures, and the only result here.

Where these numbers come from
  • The model — bert-base-uncased, 12 layers × 12 heads (Devlin et al. 2019, Apache 2.0). Every weight shown is read straight out of the model by _scripts/build_attention.py; nothing is hand-drawn.
  • Why BERT and not GPT — in a causal model, Harry cannot attend forward to Potter, because that word has not been read yet. BERT is bidirectional, so every token sees every other and the disambiguation is visible where you would look for it.
  • The similarity — cosine between the same token's hidden state in the two sentences, at each of the 13 layers (0 = the input embedding, before any attention). The control word is one that means the same thing in both sentences and sits in the same position.
  • Attention parked on [SEP] — a known and documented behaviour, not an artefact of these sentences: many heads use it as a no-op (Clark et al. 2019).
  • Everything drifts apart — representations become more context-specific with depth for every token, ambiguous or not (Ethayarajh 2019). That is why the control line is on the chart: the gap between the two lines is the claim, not the fall of either one.
  • Attention is not explanation — where a head looks is not the same as why the model answered as it did (Jain & Wallace 2019). Read this tool as a picture of the mechanism, not as evidence about a particular output.
