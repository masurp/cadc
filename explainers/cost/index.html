Where these numbers come from
  • Claude prices — Fable 5.1 $10/$50, Opus 5 $5/$25, Sonnet 5 $2/$10, Haiku 4.5 $1/$5 per million tokens in/out. claude.com/pricing, checked 8 Sep 2026.
  • OpenAI prices — GPT‑6 Astra $10/$50 (released 3 Sep 2026), GPT‑5.6 Sol $5/$30, Terra $2/$12, Luna $0.20/$1.20. BenchLM, aipricing.guru, Sep 2026.
  • Gemini prices — 3.1 Pro $2/$12; 3.8 Flash $0.75/$3.75 introductory, rising to $1.50/$7.50 on 1 Jan 2027; 3.5 Flash‑Lite $0.30/$2.50. BenchLM, CloudZero, Sep 2026.
  • Batch discount — 50% off input and output, asynchronous, up to 24 h. All three vendors.
  • Cached input — a repeated prefix is discounted ~90% by all three: Claude reads $0.50 / $0.20 / $0.10 (Fable 5.1 $0.25) (Anthropic); GPT‑5.6 Sol $0.50, Terra $0.20, Luna $0.02 (Elser); Gemini implicit caching at ~10% of input, e.g. 3.1 Pro $0.20 (Google). Assumes 90% of the repeated prefix hits.
  • Local model sizes and speed — Qwen 3.5 (the 4B, 9B, 27B and 35B‑A3B variants fit a student machine) at Q4 quantisation. Unsloth, InsiderLLM, llmhardware.io, 2026. Order‑of‑magnitude; throughput varies with quantisation and context length.
  • Time, hosted — eight requests in parallel at roughly 40 generated tokens/second for the frontier tier, 85 for the workhorse tier, 150 for the cheap tier.
  • Time, local — one request at a time, at the reference GPU's throughput scaled to the machine (0.30 for a 16 GB laptop, 0.40 for 32 GB, 1.0 for the RTX 4090, 0.75 for a Mac Studio M5), prompt read ten times faster than the answer is written. Usable memory 11 / 24 / 22 / 72 GB — less than the sticker figure, because the OS keeps some, and on Apple silicon the GPU may address only part of unified memory. A model whose weights exceed that runs roughly ten times slower, spilling to system memory. Power draw 38 / 50 / 350 / 120 W sustained. The Mac Studio figures are an estimate for a machine of that class, not a benchmark.
  • Energy per token, hosted models — anchored on a median ≈0.3 Wh for a ~500‑token GPT‑4o query (Jegham et al., “How Hungry is AI?”, 2025), with optimised serving 4–20× below widely cited estimates (Joule, 2026). Reading a token is charged at a tenth of writing one, so the workhorse tier calibrates to ≈1.6 Wh per 1,000 generated tokens, the frontier tier ≈3.2, the cheap tier ≈0.5. The weakest numbers here — an order of magnitude, not a measurement. No vendor publishes per‑request energy, which is itself the point.
  • Energy on your own machine — not looked up but computed: power draw × generation time. 45 W sustained for an Apple‑silicon laptop, 350 W for an RTX 4090 under load.
  • Electricity — €0.154/kWh, Dutch non‑household consumers, Dec 2025. GlobalPetrolPrices / Eurostat.
  • Carbon intensity — 279 gCO2eq/kWh, Dutch grid, May 2025–Apr 2026. Electricity Maps. Applied to hosted models too, because vendors do not disclose which grid served your request — which is itself the finding.
  • The comparisons — a kettle boil is 0.20 kWh (1.7 L raised 85 K at ~85% efficiency, computed, not looked up); a phone charge ≈0.012 kWh; a Dutch household uses ≈2,500 kWh a year, so ≈6.8 kWh a day (Keuze); a new car in the EU emits 96.7 gCO2/km (EEA, 2025).
  • Coder wage — scale SA, CAO Nederlandse Universiteiten 2026–27 (hourly = monthly ÷ 38 × 12/52; +3.1% from 1 Jul 2026; 8.3% year‑end bonus). UNL.
  • Currency — converted at $1 = €0.86 (EUR/USD 1.163, 8 Sep 2026). Trading Economics.
Prices checked 8 September 2026. Model prices move; if a figure here disagrees with the vendor's page, the vendor is right and this tool is stale — which is the reproducibility problem in miniature.
