Week 1 · Word frequencies

Tokeniser sandbox

Turning a pile of text into a table you can count takes a chain of small decisions. In this sandbox you make them yourself and watch the counts change.

What to do · 6 min

  1. Read the default text, then tick remove stopwords and watch the ranking rebuild.
  2. Try light stemming, then switch to bigrams.
  3. Push the rare-word cutoff up until the table looks tidy, then read how many terms that deleted.

What you take away

  • Why the is usually the most frequent word, and what removing it costs.
  • That preprocessing is a chain of decisions, each one changing the number you would report.

What to notice

  • On the default text the tops the table at 3. Tick remove stopwords and the ranking changes completely: the “most frequent word” was an artefact of a choice.
  • Light stemming merges covers and coverage into one row. Convenient, and now you cannot tell them apart.
  • Switch to bigrams or trigrams: the vocabulary multiplies while nearly every count drops to 1. This is why n-gram models need far more data.
  • Drop terms appearing fewer than N times is standard practice for sparsity, and it silently deletes evidence. Watch the “N terms removed” line.
