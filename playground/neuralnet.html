Week 3 · Neural networks

Inside a small neural network

The network from the lecture, as a tool you can walk through and train yourself. It has 69 numbers and learns from twenty short film reviews. Every sum, probability, loss and slope on the page is computed from the weights you see, for the review you choose.

What to do · 25 min

  1. Step through the 21 steps with Next. Hover over a unit or a line to read its number, and drag the drawing to turn it.
  2. In the backpropagation steps, click a line in the drawing and move the slider. Compare the slope you find by trying with the slope the tool computes.
  3. In Epoch after epoch, press Train. Then set the learning rate to 0.05 and to 30, and the hidden units to 1, and train again each time.
  4. Go back to the lecture’s settings, train to epoch 65, and type never boring and not dull into the box at the top.

What you take away

  • What happens between input and output: counts, weighted sums, an activation function, a probability.
  • How a network learns: a loss, a slope for every weight from backpropagation, and many small steps downhill.
  • What the settings of mlp() from the lecture stand for: hidden units, epochs, learning rate.

What to notice

A unit multiplies, adds and bends. Each count is multiplied by a weight, the results are added up with a bias, and the sum goes through an S-shaped curve. The first part is the sum from multiply, then add. The output unit does the same with the numbers of the hidden layer, so seen from there it is a logistic regression.

Before training, the network guesses. The weights start as random numbers. For Not good at all the untrained network gives p(positive) = 0.76, and it has 10 of the 20 reviews on the right side of 0.5. The loss puts a number on this. For this review it is −log(0.24) = 1.44, and the average over all twenty is 0.79.

Backpropagation is bookkeeping from the back. The error at the output is p(positive) minus the human label. A line into the output gets the error times the number that came over it. A hidden unit gets a share of the error, and a line into that unit gets the share times the word’s count. The check in the last backpropagation step shows that this gives the same slope as changing the weight a little and reading the loss again.

The learning rate decides how the walk goes. On the loss landscape the walk with η = 0.6 is still on its way after 40 steps, η = 6 has arrived, and η = 22 jumps from one side of the valley to the other without settling. In training, η = 0.05 leaves the network at 15 of 20 after 300 epochs, and with η = 30 the loss jumps above 10 and stays there.

The hidden layer earns its place on two reviews. Not good at all is negative and Not boring at all is positive. With the lecture’s settings all twenty reviews are right from epoch 65 on, and unit C sends −0.80 for the first of the two and +0.38 for the second. With one hidden unit the network stops at 19 of 20, and the review it misses is Not boring at all.

Twenty out of twenty is a training result. At epoch 65 the network gives never boring a p(positive) of 0.83. It gives not dull 0.20, because no training review combines these two words. How a classifier does on texts it has not seen is what a test set measures, and what how much freedom is too much is about.

What the toy leaves out. The network fitted in the lecture has 1,000 inputs, 16 hidden units and about sixteen thousand weights. It takes its steps on batches of the training data and pays a penalty for large weights. The language models of week 4 stack many such layers: the feed-forward part of every block in inside a small language model is a network of this kind.

The network has learned which combinations of fifteen words go with which label in twenty reviews. Whether a trained classifier measures what you want to measure is a question for validation.
