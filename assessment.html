The block widths are the weights. All three must be passed with a 5.5 or higher; a fail in one cannot be compensated by the other two. The final grade is the weighted average, rounded to the nearest half point: 5.5 or higher becomes a 6.0, anything below 5.5 a 5.0. Every rule in full is in the course manual (PDF).

REQUIRED TO PASS

TWO THINGS WITHOUT A GRADE

  • At least six of the eight check-ins. Each lab opens with a ten-minute check-in, on your own and without AI. It is not graded, only recorded, so a missed lab is a missed check-in. You may miss two labs, with a reasonable justification (illness, family or another serious reason): tell your lab teacher the same week.
  • All four homework assignments. A homework you do not hand in cannot be averaged away.

THE EXAM · 40%

Week 5 · in the room · on TestVision · no AI

What the exam tests

Multiple-choice and open questions on everything from weeks 1 to 4, lectures and labs alike. You read output and say what it means, find the leak in a pipeline, explain why a κ is impossible given a confusion matrix, choose a method and say what you would lose. Your R skills are tested by reading code you are shown, never by writing a script from scratch. Many questions use the data, the code and the traps from the labs: if you did those yourself, you have practised exactly this. The check-ins are your chance to practise similar questions in the room, and to ask your lab teacher for clarification on anything you do not understand.

THE HOMEWORK · 30%

Weeks 1 to 4 · four assignments · individual

One a week, started in the Thursday lab

Each homework is the second part of that week’s Lab B: started in the room, finished at home, handed in on Sunday as one rendered file with your code, three short reflections and an AI disclosure. Graded Excellent, Good, Sufficient or Insufficient; your homework grade is the mean of the four, not rounded, and must be 5.5 or higher. An Insufficient is not redone: one or two can be made up by the others, three most likely cannot.

THE GROUP PROJECT · 30%

Weeks 6 to 8 · one grade per group

Your own study, start to finish

A question of your own, data you obtained yourselves, and a measurement you validated, presented at the mini conference in week 8. A project without a validation section cannot pass, and a failed project cannot be resat.

PRACTICALITIES

How it works
Deadlines Homework is due on the Sunday after the week it belongs to, 23:59, on Canvas. Every date is on the Weeks page.
Late work Talk to us before the deadline: message the coordinator on Canvas. Without prior agreement, a late homework is graded Insufficient.
Resit Only the exam has a resit, in the resit exam week in January/February. The homework and the project are coursework and cannot be resat: a failed project means the course is failed.
Language Anything you write out yourself (homework, open exam questions, the project report) may be in English or Dutch. Multiple-choice questions, check-ins and all course materials are in English; the talk and the poster at the conference must be in English.
Feedback Check-ins are discussed in the room. Each homework gets a grade and a few lines from your lab teacher. The project gets its feedback live, at the conference.
Questions about a grade Your lab teacher first, the coordinator second. The formal route is the Examination Board, and it stays open to you.
