Week 4 · Lab B

04 VALIDATE Lab B · Validation study Week 4 · 105 min

Over four weeks you have worked with four generations of method: counting, dictionaries, classifiers and language models. The lecture put them side by side on one gold standard and asked which measurement you would defend. In this lab you make that comparison yourself: first on the Dutch headlines of van Atteveldt et al. (2021), adding the language-model row their paper could not have had, and then, in the homework, on the tweets you coded in week 2, with your own codebook as the prompt.

Part 1 · The row the paper could not add

1 · One gold set for every method

The paper’s 284 test headlines are the gold standard. To keep the language model runs short, we draw 100 of them, once, and every method is scored on exactly these 100.

library(tidyverse)
library(tidymodels)
library(textrecipes)
library(discrim)
library(tidyllm)

labels <- c("negative", "neutral", "positive")

eco <- read_csv("ecosent-headlines.csv") |>
  mutate(label = factor(label, levels = labels))

train <- filter(eco, set == "train")

set.seed(4)
gold <- eco |>
  filter(set == "test") |>
  slice_sample(n = 100)

gold |> count(label)

3 · Row 1: the classifier from week 3

The first row is the Naive Bayes classifier from the solution of week 3’s Lab A, Part 2 (the 2,000 most frequent lemmata), trained on the training headlines and scored on the 100 gold headlines. We also time it.

t0 <- Sys.time()

rec <- recipe(label ~ lemmata, data = train) |>
  step_tokenize(lemmata) |>
  step_tokenfilter(lemmata, max_tokens = 2000) |>
  step_tf(lemmata)

nb <- workflow() |>
  add_recipe(rec) |>
  add_model(naive_Bayes(Laplace = 1) |>
              set_engine("naivebayes", usepoisson = TRUE)) |>
  fit(train)

gold <- gold |>
  mutate(pred_nb = predict(nb, gold)$.pred_class)

time_nb <- Sys.time() - t0

4 · Row 2: a language model, with a schema

The prompt states the paper’s codebook in a few sentences. It is shorter than the prompt of the study on Monday’s slides, so expect other numbers than that figure. The model gets no training data at all.

codebook <- "You are coding headlines from Dutch newspapers for economic sentiment,
as in a content analysis of economic news. Codebook: positive = the headline
indicates that the economy or economic conditions are good or improving;
negative = bad or worsening; neutral = the headline makes no evaluation of
economic conditions, or is not about the economy. The headline is in Dutch.
Code the following headline.

Headline: {text}"

schema <- tidyllm_schema(name = "coding",
  label = field_fct("the code", .levels = labels))

code_schema <- function(text) {
  llm_message(str_replace(codebook, stringr::fixed("{text}"), text)) |>
    chat(ollama(.model = "llama3.2:3b", .temperature = 0, .json_schema = schema)) |>
    get_reply_data() |>
    pluck("label")
}
# with the course key, here and in section 5:
# chat(openai(.model = "gpt-5.4-nano", .temperature = 0, ...))

t0 <- Sys.time()
gold <- gold |>
  mutate(pred_llm_schema = map_chr(headline, code_schema, .progress = TRUE),
         pred_llm_schema = factor(pred_llm_schema, levels = labels))
time_llm_schema <- Sys.time() - t0

5 · Row 3: the same prompt, in free text

This row uses the same model and the same prompt, but the model answers in its own words. We then have to find the label in the reply. The parser below takes the label word that appears first, and returns NA if there is none, so that an unreadable reply stays visible instead of silently becoming a label.

code_text <- function(text) {
  llm_message(str_replace(codebook, stringr::fixed("{text}"), text)) |>
    chat(ollama(.model = "llama3.2:3b", .temperature = 0)) |>
    get_reply()
}

t0 <- Sys.time()
gold <- gold |>
  mutate(reply = map_chr(headline, code_text, .progress = TRUE),
         pred_llm_text = str_extract(str_to_lower(reply), "negative|neutral|positive"),
         pred_llm_text = factor(pred_llm_text, levels = labels))
time_llm_text <- Sys.time() - t0

sum(is.na(gold$pred_llm_text))                 # replies without a label
gold |> select(headline, reply) |> head(3)

Part 2 · Homework 4: your own measurement

Open week04-lab-b-part2.qmd from the lab folder, and copy your gold-corona.csv from week 2 next to it. Use the rest of the session to get your codebook running as a prompt, and finish the rest at home. This is the last homework.

In week 2 you and your partner wrote a codebook, coded 50 #corona tweets, and measured your agreement. In Homework 2 you tested the Bing dictionary against that gold standard. Now the codebook you wrote for a human becomes the prompt for a language model.

Your question: Which measurement of the sentiment of your 50 tweets would you defend: the dictionary, the language model, or neither?

The skeleton takes you through eight steps:

  1. Load the packages and your gold standard.
  2. Turn your week 2 codebook into a prompt, with the rules you added after reading your disagreements.
  3. Version 1: run the model with a schema on all 50 tweets, and time it.
  4. Version 2: run the same prompt and model in free text, find the code in each reply with the parser the skeleton gives you, and time it.
  5. Give each tweet the dictionary’s verdict, with your code from Homework 2.
  6. Make one table: your partner, the dictionary, both model versions and the majority baseline, with accuracy, κ, replies without a label, and time.
  7. List the tweets on which version 1 and your gold standard disagree.
  8. Fill in the six reporting lines from the lecture.

There is no trained classifier in the table: there is no training data for these tweets. Then three short reflections on your own results, and your AI disclosure.
